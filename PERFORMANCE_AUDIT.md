## Scraping, coverage and retailer data use

### Discovery and traffic

- **Scope and query translation.** Live `SOURCES` contains SpaceNet and Scoop (`scraping/discovery.py:29–31`). Static category/brand/gaming seeds differ per adapter (`scraping/spacenet.py:1–5`, `scraping/scoop.py:1–12`). Mice lack direct seeds and can incur homepage discovery. The canonical vocabulary expands English/French/Arabic terms and filters accessory wording (`backend/services/shopping_terms.py`). FTS uses alias first-token prefixes before exact heuristics. In the live worker, known URLs are rechecked first, category links come from numeric home menu paths, and one category is chosen by lexical feature/brand scoring. Public retailer query endpoints are outside the active lookup path. [Scoop mouse category](https://www.scoop.com.tn/36-souris) · [SpaceNet Logitech mouse category](https://spacenet.tn/1155-souris-logitech)
- **Recall bounds.** Per retailer the worker tests up to two known URLs, one category, two pages and six sitemap files with at most seven distinct product-page attempts and a target of four accepted observations (`discovery.py:67–75,99–138,150–235`). Sitemaps match slugs, require `.html`, and can filter out candidates whose brand or RGB is absent from the slug even if present on the PDP. The collection prioritizes availability and feature text and spends requests before confirming PDP price. In a controlled test with fresh robots and HTML, seven 499 TND mice on page one occupied seven PDP slots and ten HTTP calls, hiding a permitted 100 TND mouse on page two. A known URL returning 302 aborted after two requests and a category returning 404 aborted after three, before a valid sitemap fallback. A parsed 400.000 TND item counted as saved for “under 400” because discovery checks `<=`. API search excluded it using the strict bound (`discovery.py:118–129`, `shopping.py:234–238`).
- **Pacing and retries.** `get_page` uses identified `TechStockAcademicBot/1.0`, disables redirect following and retries, and serializes host start times through SQLite `RetailerClock` with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`. Spacing is at least 10 s for SpaceNet and 2 s for Scoop at defaults, subject to robots and configuration. Each HTTP GET has up to 15 s, clipped against a nominal 95 s per-source deadline for discovery, with a 1 s minimum. HTTP 401/403/429/503 pause the host for at least 300 s or longer per valid Retry-After. Product 404/410 marks the stored stock unknown and continues. Other exceptions stop that source and preserve accumulated observations when available. Source workers run concurrently across retailers and fetch serially within a lookup. Across lookups the shared clock paces start times, so slow requests can overlap. Waits block executor threads, and SQLite lock waits and in-flight synchronous calls can extend beyond coroutine cancellation or nominal deadline checks (`scraping/public_pages.py:16–77`, `discovery.py:67–103,237–272`).
- **Repeat requests and browsers.** The batch path sets concurrency 1 and can follow next links up to `SCRAPING_MAX_PAGES` (default 20). Scheduled collection usually requests five entries per category and retailer, with exceptions such as Scoop earphones 18, accessories 25 and keyboards 8, and gaming-laptop sweeps of up to 96 SpaceNet and 40 Scoop entries at defaults (`shopping.py:159–225`). In a paced Scoop fixture, one laptop took 6.117 s wall vs 4.091 s in the batch report. Four actual requests were manual robots, Scrapling robots, listing and product, starting at ~0/2/4/6 s, while `pages_visited` reported 2. Each refresh call independently fetches robots. Live caches robots for 10 min per process and sitemaps for 24 h in SQLite, with no shared category/home HTML cache. JavaScript rendering is off by default and only wired into batch fallback after missing listing selectors or missing product JSON-LD. Each fallback starts fresh Chromium after the initial HTTP request. Browser navigation leaves the shared GET clock and HTTP counters, and the renderer checks the top-level host before and after navigation without intercepting subresources. Browser launch and retailer JS performance were unavailable to measure (`refresh_catalog.py:74–127,151–199`, `scraping/render.py:1–19`, `discovery.py:48–91`).
- **Duplicate work and freshness.** Normal search takes a warm path if it finds any recent returned card (6 h default), except for explicit refresh or requested multi-store comparison with fewer than two represented stores. Search eligibility is 48 h by default. Same-signature live searches share a SQLite lease for 115 s on claim, then 30 s after forced or 90 s after ordinary attempts. Criteria are hashed together, including budget and comparison inputs, so different criteria can repeat retrieval of the same pages. A failure can suppress an immediate retry. Per verified URL, streaming does a new full catalog search and emits a full product snapshot, plus final retrieval. Periodic refresh runs on a separate 24 h schedule with a 4 h lease. It can record completion when only some individual calls succeeded. Batch and live writes update existing records and timestamps, with no universal invalidation for offers omitted from a sample (`shopping.py:586–634`, `discovery.py:245–272`, `catalog_refresh.py:19–72`).

### Extraction, identity, price and stock

- `scraping/parser.py:23–69` requires JSON-LD `Product`, exactly one offer when represented as a list, explicit TND currency and category-compatible name. It extracts the data-sheet and attribute-table selectors, first image, normalized specs, source ID and URL. It stores the raw description, attributes and Product metadata. No universal HTML-only price fallback or DOM/JSON-LD reconciliation is present. Parsing the recorded `tests/fixtures/spacenet_product.html` with a reused selector, 300 local parses yielded **0.356 ms median / 0.403 ms p95** before I/O and DB writes. Batch report rejection counts above demonstrate that successful HTTP responses alone do not guarantee useful records.
- `scraping/normalize.py:18–27,60–64` maps source price and availability strings. Controlled tests found `-59.900` became positive `59.900`. “Non disponible,” “Disponible sur commande” and “Disponible uniquement en magasin” became `in_stock`. Schema.org `PreOrder` and `LimitedAvailability` stayed unknown. Page stock can be branch-specific. The observed catalog had no populated quantities, and the shared parser leaves quantities null. Even when a timestamp is recent, the remote page is the source observation. Taxes, delivery charges, branch-level availability and checkout confirmation are outside the parser's price/stock comparison.
- In `scraping/refresh_catalog.py:17–38` each retailer offer has stable storage identity by canonical URL or retailer/source ID, separate from display grouping in `backend/services/price_comparison.py:13–74`. Display identity checks checksum-valid GTIN, brand/MPN with configuration and color guards, exact normalized brand/model/title, then product ID. Same MPN grouped different French/English G502 titles in the paced fixture. Similar titles without reliable identifiers split in a separate controlled check. Identical valid GTINs with conflicting colors grouped. GTIN takes precedence over the MPN configuration guard. Grouping selects the lowest eligible offer and uses decimal TND price differences. `backend/services/catalog.py:47–69` removes common image extensions from serialized product links and retains the image URL separately.

## AI, intent and conversational state

- **Call graph.** Cart commands, recognized follow-ups, named-model pairs and the narrow mouse/phone/RAM-GPU fast path run locally. Other configured turns complete one planner decision that waits for the provider before database/discovery work starts. `SearchProducts`, `CompareShown` and `GeneralAnswer` are Pydantic schemas bound together with one tool call required. The SDK uses a 25 s timeout, zero retries, no model token streaming and current configured routing via `openrouter/free`. OpenAI is also supported. Failure, content-only output, unexpected action count or invalid schema fall back to local rules. The planner is one call with at most one selected action. Named-pair search can start two concurrent listing lookups, each able to start both retailer workers. Generic laptops under 3,000 TND enter the model branch when configured even though offline fallback found 12 local cards. Narrow deterministic intent avoids that delay for recognized mouse requests (`shopping.py:500–582,690–740`, `shopping_intent.py:39–66`, `shopping_agent.py:16–86`, `llm.py:6–18`).
- **Provider timing and payload.** Three configured OpenRouter requests used synthetic inputs: English laptop recommendation, French Bluetooth headset search and an English two-card GPU comparison. All failed before HTTP with connection errors in **106.48, 5.22, 5.24 ms**. DNS independently gave macOS `gaierror`. These are failure timings. Thirty warm calls per shape through the real SDK intercepted with HTTPX MockTransport measured body sizes of **5,191 B** empty, **6,345 B** with two recent messages and cards, **12,048 B** with twelve short messages/cards and **58,188 B** with twelve fuller entries/cards. The three schemas contributed **3,470 B** each request collectively. Local mock medians were 2.85–3.27 ms. Serialized bytes measure transfer size, and provider token counts, successful inference latency and cost remain unknown.
- **Context and grounding.** Memory is owner-scoped and reads the last 12 individual messages, 800 characters each, active criteria and card IDs. It recovers cards from the latest stored result containing products and sends their attributes without a combined token or bytes budget. Numeric comparisons use actual card fields. A features comparison can show a model-selected position with no validation of its justification. Stored cards retain previous prices/stock and comparison does not fetch retailer pages. `GeneralAnswer` is rendered as model text and relies on prompt-only limits against unverified prices, stock and links. Catalog search responses use source-backed product cards and code templates. Stock and price trust inherits parser and retailer-data limits. Separate read, execution and write phases let overlapping turns in one conversation share prior context. The last completion controls the persisted active filters (`shopping.py:428–441,687–752`, `shopping_agent.py:36–61,64–118`).
- **Recommendation behavior.** Ordinary results use lexical relevance, preferences and price. `sort=recommend` adds published capability features, value, age and a small signal based on recent HTTP success. GPU priority uses a hand-maintained tier table. A controlled comparison ranked `RTX 4060 Ti` at 88, while `RTX 4060Ti`, Radeon RX 7900 and Intel Arc A770 were unknown-tier and omitted from the numeric GPU comparison when other recognized GPUs exist. The offline programming-laptop request returned cards ordered by price, led by an 8 GB/256 GB Chuwi, with no suitability assessment. The previous image-URL/explicit keyboard-and-mouse-pack symptom did not recur on the exact mouse replay. Controlled alternative titles `Clavier + Souris Gamer` and `Souris Gamer avec tapis` still matched (`shopping.py:245–293,630–645`, `catalog.py:13–37,47–69`, `shopping_agent.py:89–118`, `shopping_terms.py:89–125`).

## Backend, storage and scaling

- **SQLite.** `backend/database.py:10–24` supports SQLite only, enables WAL and foreign keys per connection and sets 30 s SQLite busy wait. Startup initializes tables and FTS5 inside an immediate write transaction. The FTS5 external-content table and insert/update/delete triggers maintain titles transactionally (`backend/services/title_index.py:8–34`). Product category and Offer product ID indexes support candidate selection. Offer stock, age and price filters rely on the query plan around those candidates and have no dedicated composite index in the model (`backend/models.py:14–45`). Schema also stores host clocks, search leases, sitemap JSON and conversation/card snapshots. Six sitemap rows held **2,336,470 JSON bytes** in a **3,375,104-byte** backup, about 69% by size comparison. Sidecar checkpoints and WAL have separate disk footprints.
- **Candidate load.** Modern search uses one SELECT with eager Product join. It materializes and serializes matching candidates, applies Python feature predicates, groups and ranks everything before slicing to 12. Empty-result diagnostics can run extra queries, including a full offer scan for brand availability. Legacy `/catalog` and MCP search each executed 127 SELECTs on 126 offers because of lazy Product reads after a full offer scan. The MCP 100-result cap is applied after filtering. Legacy selection preparation repeats the initial search and comparison to construct a trusted snapshot (`shopping.py:228–329`, `catalog.py:87–96`, `backend/main.py:216–218`, `backend/services/selection.py:13–24`).
- **Blocking and state.** Modern search runs via `asyncio.to_thread`, with synchronous history/cart reads and final SQLite writes still on the async loop. A disposable SQLite connection held `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` for 350 ms while serving cached `/shopping/chat`. In three trials the chat completed in **369.69–373.61 ms**. A same-loop `/health` scheduled for +20 ms only resumed at **368.71–372.63 ms**. This isolated contention case shows that the write can block unrelated requests on a worker. Conversation list and detail endpoints return all matching rows, with embedded product snapshots in detail. History is indexed by owner or conversation ID, with the list sorting by updated time (`shopping.py:360–370,428–441,741–752`). The 349-conversation test list response measured 49,483 B and 5.97/6.20 ms median/p95. `/health` was 0.29/0.38 ms, `/shopping/cart` 1.09/1.27 ms and legacy `/catalog` 25.89/27.55 ms and 159,337 B without contention.
- **Worker topology and failures.** Default local/Docker launch uses one Uvicorn worker. Each FastAPI lifespan initializes one graph, MCP subprocess and checkpoint saver (~663 ms for the observed local lifespan start). The legacy process lock holds across model/MCP awaits on chat, structured requests, approval and selection. MCP timeout is 30 s per call, with two bounded transient graph retries and a capped step budget. Scheduled collection and many distinct live queries can occupy thread capacity even while waiting on host pacing. The app reports basic health independently of provider, retailer and database readiness. No sustained multi-worker or mixed external-load test was available (`Dockerfile:7`, `scripts/run.py:21–24`, `backend/main.py:24–38,166–168,191–214,236–244,263–279`, `mcp_server/client.py:7–16`, `agent/graph.py:24–39`).
- **NDJSON and counters.** `/shopping/chat/stream` sends `progress`, `result` or `error` after HTTP 200 headers. The same invalid conversation returns 404 on JSON chat. The stream has no heartbeat or bounded progress queue and cancels the async runner on exit. Synchronous source workers can continue until completion/deadline. An ASGI fixture that verified two URLs 175 ms apart produced full snapshots at 4.14/191.69/371.00 ms and a final at 380.22 ms. Per-frame sizes 10,847/11,309/11,307/11,959 B totaled **45,422 B**, 3.8× the final alone. Each callback reloads/searches/ranks before emission. Metrics accumulate process-local totals with no histograms, provider-stage timing or initial/live lookup breakdown in the retrieval counter. A cache hit means nonempty initial local search. A handled provider or retailer failure can still be a successful turn. Retailer HTTP-OK metrics also count robots/listing responses regardless of usable records. Optional LangSmith can instrument paths with payload hiding configured by default (`backend/main.py:100–138,153–159`, `shopping.py:586–625,669–684`, `shopping_metrics.py:9–101`, `backend/config.py:3–7`).

## React and Streamlit performance

- **Build and assets.** One scratch Vite 7.3.8 production build with React 19.3.1 and Node 26.13 processed 27 modules in **501 ms**. Output was 377 B HTML, 250,213 B JS (~78.55 kB gzip), and 13,315 B CSS (~3.78 kB gzip). No route-level chunks. Docker Compose runs Vite dev and installs dependencies before copying the lockfile, so its served assets and resolved versions can differ (`frontend/package.json`, `frontend/Dockerfile:1–6`, `docker-compose.yml:31–40`). Results lazy-load images directly from retailer origins. The cart uses eager images and CSS imports Google Fonts. Font and image timing was outside the build measurement.
- **Network and state.** Authenticated boot awaits `/shopping/auth/me` then starts conversation list and cart in parallel. Each submit opens one NDJSON POST and then awaits an additional full conversation-list GET before the composer becomes ready. The stream uses a 240 s timeout and no user cancel action in the composer. Rendering maps full conversation lists and messages and recreates product-card elements on UI state changes. Progress snapshots and messages also trigger smooth scroll. Product cards format per-offer price/date fields and render comparison tables. In controlled repros, a 503 from the refresh GET after a successful result appended a misleading assistant retry, a slower earlier conversation GET overrode a later click, and two rapid cart increments both submitted absolute quantity 2. A startup history or cart request failure enters the common catch and clears the local session (`frontend/src/main.jsx:11–47,53–75,144–201`).
- **DOM timing.** Production bundle in JSDOM 27.0.1, Node 26.13, synthetic fetch and NDJSON. Ten post-warmup trials measured signed-out shell **3.6/4.4 ms** median/p95. Authenticated shell **23.5/24.6 ms** with mocked 15 ms auth delay. Sidebar **43.6/45.5 ms** with an added parallel 20 ms history/cart delay. Submit-to-pending **2.2/2.6 ms**. Fragmented preview visible in DOM **44.9/46.4 ms** with last byte scheduled at 30 ms. Final cards **79.7/82.4 ms** for a 65 ms scheduled result. Composer ready **93.2/95.4 ms** with an extra 20 ms history request. On history open with zero injected delay and two offers/card, seven warm runs gave **14.4/15.5 ms** for 12 cards (545 chat DOM elements), **86.0/89.1 ms** for 120 (5,441 nodes) and **412.0/578.7 ms** for 600 (27,201 nodes). These are JS/DOM timings, excluding native layout, paint, network and image decode.
- **Browser and legacy UI.** Browser benchmark could not launch in the sandbox. The expected Playwright Chromium revision was absent and a locally installed alternate Chromium was denied by the Mach rendezvous permission check. Local socket binding also failed. Streamlit AppTest with mocked backend covered two legacy UI tests, with both passing in 0.98 s in isolation. Legacy Streamlit reruns use synchronous HTTP (`app/ui.py:11–20`). With seven warmed tests and mocked API, all-match catalog render took **5.6/5.7, 23.4/23.9 and 105.0/124.9 ms** at 12, 120 and 600 rows. The zero-match filter still retrieved the full catalog and took **3.6/4.1, 5.8/6.5 and 15.0/15.3 ms** at those sizes (`app/pages/2_Product_Catalog.py:3–21`).
